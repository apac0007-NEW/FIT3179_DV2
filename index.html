From neighbourhood celebrations to cultural festivals, shared events create chances to meet, contribute and feel part of a place. National surveys reveal who takes part, where participation differs, and where the desire to connect is still unmet.

THE QUESTION

What helps a community come together?

Festival programs are local, but the conditions that shape participation are national: access to culture, opportunities to volunteer, and whether people feel connected to their neighbours.

This story combines nationally representative survey evidence with state and territory comparisons. It distinguishes festival attendance from broader cultural participation and from general measures of social connection—related ideas, but not interchangeable measures.

01

The national festival footprint is uneven

Creative Australia mapped 535 music festivals held in 2022–23. Comparing raw counts with festivals per 10,000 residents shows why a state’s total and its relative density tell different stories. This national dataset gives a reliable festival-specific slice, although it does not include every neighbourhood celebration or council event.

02

Culture is a shared activity—but not everyone joins in the same way

Age and gender differences show why community events benefit from varied formats, timings and ways to participate.

The mirrored layout compares men and women in the same age bands. It measures cultural activities broadly, rather than festival attendance alone.

The heatmap makes the participation gap visible across age groups without requiring viewers to compare many separate bars.

Children’s creative participation was lower in several activities in 2021–22 than in 2017–18. The survey period overlapped the COVID-19 pandemic, so the comparison is descriptive rather than causal.

03

Festivals draw people in—and many want more opportunities

National arts participation research gives a direct view of festival attendance and shows that demand is not fully met, particularly outside major cities.

This is the latest national attendance/access measure. The three rows represent different questions: attendance, cost as the biggest barrier, and missing wanted events because of cost.

In the 2022 survey, 64% of people in remote locations said they would like to attend more cultural and artistic events. This older regional breakdown complements the 2025 national attendance findings; it is not proof that festivals alone would close the gap.

These 2025 measures capture participation through cultural identity, attendance at First Nations arts events/festivals and attitudes toward First Nations arts. They are not counts of festivals held.

04

Connection takes people power

Behind public celebrations are people giving time, while broader neighbourhood indicators show why opportunities to meet and contribute matter.

The overall volunteering figure uses cultural participants as its base; the performance-specific figures use participants in those activities. These percentages are not directly additive.

The neighbourhood measures are from 2024; the arts and cultural measures are from 2022. They describe related aspects of community life but do not establish that festivals caused connection.

These gender and geography comparisons are from the 2022 survey. Lower participation should not be interpreted as lower interest.

This organiser survey had 51 responses and covers music festivals, not every community event. It highlights pressures relevant to councils and local organisers.

WHAT THE EVIDENCE SUGGESTS

Make it easier to show up—and easier to help.

Bring events closer. The stronger desire for more cultural experiences in remote communities points to a need to consider travel, local programming and regional access.

Design for different lives. Participation varies by age and gender, so one event format will not suit every audience. Family-friendly, accessible, low-cost and culturally relevant options widen the invitation.

Support the people behind the program. Cultural volunteering is part of the participation ecosystem. Clear roles, flexible shifts and practical support can make it easier for residents to contribute.

These findings show patterns and opportunities, not a causal test of festival impact. A nationwide, consistently defined inventory of local-council and neighbourhood festivals was not available in the supplied project files; this page therefore uses national survey measures rather than presenting a partial council listing as a national count.

HOW TO READ THIS

What, why, how

WHAT · Data

Creative Australia’s Soundcheck inventory maps 535 music festivals in 2022–23. The latest national attendance/access measures come from the 2025 National Arts Participation Survey; selected age/gender and regional-demand details come from its 2022 predecessor. ABS cultural participation data (2021–22) and AIHW social connection indicators (2024) add context. Each measure is labelled by scope and year.

WHY · Audience and task

For Australian residents interested in local celebrations and community life. The story helps readers compare participation, see who wants more opportunities, and understand how volunteering and neighbourhood connection relate to the wider festival ecosystem.

HOW · Visual encoding

Three map idioms compare geographic patterns; a dumbbell chart compares survey waves; a mirrored chart and heatmap reveal demographic differences; a slopegraph shows change by activity; and point, lollipop and matrix charts highlight unmet demand and contribution. Tooltips support optional detail without interrupting the narrative.
